# CHRONOS research dossier

**Question.** Compare the consequences of the printing press, the spread of the internet and the emergence of generative AI. Which common economic and social mechanisms can be identified? What do these mechanisms suggest about human development by 2040?

_Assembled from stored knowledge-graph records and scenario output only. Evidence levels are curator assessments (see each reference's verification status); CHRONOS statistics do not raise them._

## 1–2. Timelines (documented events)

### Movable-type printing (c. 1450)
- **1450–1800** — Surge in European book production after 1450 _(refs: buringh_vanzanden2009)_
- **1450–1500** — Printing spreads to about 200 European cities _(refs: dittmar2011, istc)_
- **1450 ±5y** — Movable-type printing press in Mainz (Gutenberg) _(refs: dittmar2011, eisenstein1979)_
- **1500–1600** — Faster growth of early-printing cities, 1500-1600 _(refs: dittmar2011)_
- **1517** — Protestant Reformation begins (Luther, 1517) _(refs: dittmar_seabold2019, rubin2014)_

### Computers, the Internet and the Web (1970s-2010s)
- **1970–1998** — Computerisation of the US economy _(refs: autor_levy_murnane2003, jorgenson_ho_stiroh2008)_
- **1970–1998** — Decline in demand for routine tasks with computerisation _(refs: autor_levy_murnane2003)_
- **1987** — Computer productivity paradox ('everywhere but in the productivity statistics') _(refs: david1990, solow1987)_
- **1989-03** — World Wide Web proposed at CERN _(refs: berners_lee1989, cern_public_domain1993)_
- **1993-04-30** — CERN puts WWW software in the public domain _(refs: cern_public_domain1993)_
- **1995–2004 ±1y** — US productivity growth resurgence (mid-1990s to early 2000s) _(refs: jorgenson_ho_stiroh2008)_
- **2019 ±1y** — About half of the world population online _(refs: itu_ff2019)_

### Generative AI (2017/2022-)
- **2017-06** — Transformer architecture published _(refs: vaswani2017)_
- **2022-11-30** — Public launch of ChatGPT _(refs: openai_chatgpt2022, reuters_ubs2023)_
- **2023–2024** — No detectable effect on earnings or hours two years after adoption (Denmark) _(refs: humlum_vestergaard2025)_
- **2023–2025** — Experimental evidence of task-level productivity gains from generative AI _(refs: brynjolfsson_li_raymond2025, noy_zhang2023, peng2023)_
- **2024-08-01** — EU Artificial Intelligence Act enters into force _(refs: eu_ai_act2024)_

### Electrification (comparator)
- **1880–1930 ±10y** — Electrification of factories (dynamo era) _(refs: david1990)_

## 5. Documented facts

- [printing] European book production grew about 1% per year on average over the long run (manuscripts and printed books). _(ref: buringh_vanzanden2009; metadata_checked; Authors describe estimates as low estimates.)_
- [printing] About 30,000 editions printed before 1501 are recorded in the ISTC (30,518 as of August 2016). _(ref: istc; metadata_checked; Count depends on catalogue snapshot (29,244-30,518 in different listings).)_
- [printing] Presses were established in about 200 of over 1,000 historic European cities between 1450 and 1500. _(ref: dittmar2011; metadata_checked)_
- [printing] Cities with presses by 1500 grew about 60% faster in 1500-1600 than otherwise similar cities. _(ref: dittmar2011; metadata_checked; Later summaries express the effect as 20-78 percentage points depending on specification.)_
- [internet] Adjusting for intangible investment, US TFP was 15.9% above official measures at end-2017 (mainly software). _(ref: brynjolfsson_rock_syverson2021; metadata_checked)_
- [internet] About half of the world population used the internet in 2019 (ITU first estimate 54%, 4.1 billion; revised to 51%, 4.0 billion). _(ref: itu_ff2019; metadata_checked; Revised by ITU from 54% to 51%.)_
- [internet] ITU revised its 2024 estimate of internet users to 5.8 billion (71% of the world population). _(ref: itu_ff2025; metadata_checked)_
- [genai] ChatGPT was estimated to have about 100 million monthly active users in January 2023, two months after launch (UBS estimate from Similarweb data, not an OpenAI figure). _(ref: reuters_ubs2023; metadata_checked; Third-party traffic estimate.)_
- [genai] In a randomised writing-task experiment, ChatGPT cut completion time by 40% and raised quality by 18%. _(ref: noy_zhang2023; metadata_checked)_
- [genai] Developers with GitHub Copilot completed a coding task 55.8% faster (95% CI 21-89%). _(ref: peng2023; metadata_checked)_
- [genai] About 80% of US workers could have at least 10% of their tasks affected by LLMs (exposure, not realised impact). _(ref: eloundou2024; metadata_checked)_
- [genai] 39% of US adults aged 18-64 had used generative AI by August 2024. _(ref: bick_blandin_deming2024; metadata_checked; Single survey (Real-Time Population Survey, >5,000 respondents); later revision reports 'nearly 40%' for late 2024.)_
- [genai] Task-based macro estimate: AI raises TFP by no more than 0.66% over 10 years (likely below 0.53%). _(ref: acemoglu2025; metadata_checked)_
- [genai] A generative-AI assistant raised customer-support productivity by about 15% on average, more for novices. _(ref: brynjolfsson_li_raymond2025; metadata_checked; Working-paper version reported 14%.)_
- [genai] Danish administrative data rule out average effects on earnings or hours larger than 2% two years after generative-AI adoption. _(ref: humlum_vestergaard2025; metadata_checked)_

## 6. Counter-hypotheses and qualifications

- **The slow productivity payoff of computers parallels electrification: gains required reorganisation and arrived with a long lag.**
  - qualifies: gordon2016 (IT's effects smaller than earlier great inventions)
- **Generative AI lowers the time cost of producing text and code on well-defined tasks (production, not only distribution, of information becomes cheaper).**
  - alternative: Time saved on drafting may be offset by checking, correcting and new tasks created by the tool (real-job time savings appear much smaller than in experiments).
  - qualifies: humlum_vestergaard2025 (about 3% average time savings in real jobs (earlier version))
- **The spread of generative AI shaped the EU AI Act's rules on general-purpose AI models.**
  - alternative: The legislative process started before ChatGPT (Commission proposal of 2021), so the Act as a whole was not caused by generative AI; only parts may have been reshaped.
- **Two years after adoption, generative AI has not measurably changed earnings or hours in exposed occupations.**
  - alternative: Too early: complementary investment (J-curve) and reorganisation can delay effects, as with electricity and computers.
  - alternative: Denmark's institutions (wage setting, firm structure) may limit pass-through to earnings; results may not transfer to other labour markets.
  - qualifies: brynjolfsson_rock_syverson2021 (J-curve: early measured effects understate later gains)
  - qualifies: eloundou2024 (large exposure (potential) in the US)
- **Generative AI raises productivity on specific tasks (writing, customer support, coding), with larger gains for less experienced workers.**
  - alternative: External validity: short, self-contained experimental tasks may overstate gains in real jobs; output accuracy was not always verified.
  - qualifies: humlum_vestergaard2025 (average time savings about 3% in the Danish survey (earlier version))
- **Small measured effects of generative AI so far resemble the early computer productivity paradox; whether a later payoff follows is unknown.**
  - contradicts: acemoglu2025 (modest long-run effect expected)
- **After a lag, IT investment and IT-producing industries drove the US productivity acceleration of the mid-1990s.**
  - alternative: Much of the acceleration came from productivity in IT-producing sectors (semiconductors) rather than from internet use in the wider economy.
  - alternative: Cyclical factors and measurement (unmeasured intangibles) can shift the apparent timing of gains.
  - qualifies: gordon2016
- **Computerisation substituted for routine tasks and complemented non-routine analytic and interactive work.**
  - alternative: Trade and offshoring also reduced demand for routine tasks over the same period.
- **Print created a new 'print culture' (standardisation, fixity, preservation) that transformed early-modern knowledge.**
  - alternative: Fixity and credibility of print were achieved through social conventions and institutions, not by the technology itself.
  - contradicts: johns1998 (p. 19: 'Eisenstein's print culture does not exist')
- **Early adoption of printing caused faster city growth 1500-1600 (about 60% faster than similar cities).**
  - alternative: Printers may have chosen already-promising locations (selection); Dittmar reports no prior growth advantage and instruments adoption with distance from Mainz.
  - alternative: The instrument would be invalid if distance from Mainz affected growth through other channels (e.g. trade networks along the Rhine).
- **Printing lowered the cost of books, which together with rising literacy drove the post-1450 surge in book production.**
  - alternative: Rising literacy and urban demand (universities, laity) may have driven demand independently of the cost fall; the two are hard to separate.
  - qualifies: johns1998 (print's authority and fixity were constructed, not automatic)
- **Access to printing raised the likelihood that cities adopted Protestantism.**
  - alternative: Political economy of rulers and cities (fiscal and power motives) as primary drivers; printing as an amplifier rather than a cause.
- **The Transformer architecture is the technical basis of the GPT models behind ChatGPT.**
  - alternative: Compute scaling, data and reinforcement learning from human feedback were equally necessary conditions.
- **An open, royalty-free web standard contributed to internet diffusion reaching about half of humanity by 2019.**
  - alternative: Mobile phones, falling hardware prices and telecom liberalisation were probably larger drivers of the 2000s-2010s diffusion than the web's licensing.

_Claims at level ≥ 3 with no recorded counter-evidence or alternative (curation gap): c_web_open_standard_

### What the mechanisms suggest for 2040: registered hypotheses (not predictions of known accuracy)

- **oh_adoption_majority** — By 2035, a majority of employed adults in high-income countries use generative AI at work at least weekly.  
  mechanism: Falling marginal cost of reproducing and distributing information; based on claims c_genai_info_cost (L5), c_web_diffusion (L2); check by 2035; status: **NOT OPERATIONALISED (no machine-checkable indicator)**  
  falsified if: Representative surveys (e.g. the Real-Time Population Survey used by Bick, Blandin & Deming) show weekly work use below 50% of employed adults in the US or EU in 2035.
- **oh_authority_regulation** — Disputes over the authority and provenance of information intensify, and regulation keeps lagging deployment by several years.  
  mechanism: Contestation of established authorities and of information quality; based on claims c_print_reformation (L4), c_genai_regulation (L2); check by 2040; status: **NOT OPERATIONALISED (no machine-checkable indicator)**  
  falsified if: NOT YET OPERATIONALISED: requires an agreed indicator of information-authority conflict; as stated the hypothesis is too vague to be falsified.
- **oh_jcurve_lag** — Task-level gains do not translate into a large acceleration of world GDP-per-person growth before the early 2030s.  
  mechanism: Complementary intangible investment delays measured productivity gains (J-curve); based on claims c_dynamo_ict_analogy (L3), c_ict_resurgence (L4), c_genai_small_labor (L4); check by 2033; status: **PENDING**  
  falsified if: World GDP per person grows at 3.0%/yr or more on average over 2026-2031 (Maddison/OWID data).
- **oh_local_advantage** — Places and firms that adopt AI early gain a growth advantage over comparable late adopters, as early-printing cities did after 1500.  
  mechanism: Growth advantage of early-adopting places and firms; based on claims c_print_city_growth (L5); check by 2035; status: **NOT OPERATIONALISED (no machine-checkable indicator)**  
  falsified if: Credible designs (instrumented adoption or staggered roll-outs) find no productivity or growth difference between early and late adopting regions/firms by 2035.
- **oh_task_reallocation** — Through 2035, labour-market effects of generative AI show up mainly as task reallocation within occupations, not as large aggregate falls in employment or hours.  
  mechanism: Task displacement and reinstatement; based on claims c_ict_tasks (L4), c_genai_small_labor (L4); check by 2035; status: **NOT OPERATIONALISED (no machine-checkable indicator)**  
  falsified if: Administrative data show average falls in hours or earnings above 5% in the most exposed occupations relative to less exposed ones by 2032.

## 8. What follows from the evidence — and what does not

**Supported by quantitative or identified designs (in their own historical context):**
- Generative AI lowers the time cost of producing text and code on well-defined tasks (production, not only distribution, of information becomes cheaper). (L5)
- Generative AI raises productivity on specific tasks (writing, customer support, coding), with larger gains for less experienced workers. (L5)
- Early adoption of printing caused faster city growth 1500-1600 (about 60% faster than similar cities). (L5)
- Two years after adoption, generative AI has not measurably changed earnings or hours in exposed occupations. (L4)
- After a lag, IT investment and IT-producing industries drove the US productivity acceleration of the mid-1990s. (L4)
- Computerisation substituted for routine tasks and complemented non-routine analytic and interactive work. (L4)
- Printing lowered the cost of books, which together with rising literacy drove the post-1450 surge in book production. (L4)
- Access to printing raised the likelihood that cities adopted Protestantism. (L4)

**Contested (contradicting evidence recorded):**
- Print created a new 'print culture' (standardisation, fixity, preservation) that transformed early-modern knowledge.
- Small measured effects of generative AI so far resemble the early computer productivity paradox; whether a later payoff follows is unknown.

**Qualified (limits of scope or external validity recorded):**
- Generative AI lowers the time cost of producing text and code on well-defined tasks (production, not only distribution, of information becomes cheaper).
- Generative AI raises productivity on specific tasks (writing, customer support, coding), with larger gains for less experienced workers.
- Two years after adoption, generative AI has not measurably changed earnings or hours in exposed occupations.
- After a lag, IT investment and IT-producing industries drove the US productivity acceleration of the mid-1990s.
- Printing lowered the cost of books, which together with rising literacy drove the post-1450 surge in book production.
- The slow productivity payoff of computers parallels electrification: gains required reorganisation and arrived with a long lag.

**Speculative (level ≤ 2):**
- The spread of generative AI shaped the EU AI Act's rules on general-purpose AI models.
- Small measured effects of generative AI so far resemble the early computer productivity paradox; whether a later payoff follows is unknown.
- Both printing and the web sharply lowered the cost of distributing text; this is an analogy, not a causal link.
- The Transformer architecture is the technical basis of the GPT models behind ChatGPT.
- An open, royalty-free web standard contributed to internet diffusion reaching about half of humanity by 2019.

**Assumptions required for any statement about the future:**
- Mechanism transfer: that a mechanism documented for printing or the web operates similarly for generative AI is an ASSUMPTION; contexts differ (literacy, institutions, market structure, speed).
- Diffusion metrics are not commensurable across transitions (cities with presses vs share of population online vs share of US adults who tried a tool); speed comparisons are qualitative.
- Anything stated about 2040 is conditional on a scenario's assumptions; no scenario receives a probability.
